# Withdrawal eligibility at submission and payout

Pending staff/member requests previously checked ownership/account status but could bypass withdrawal product rules and current balance until payout. Approval steps could advance even when the request had become ineligible.

SavingsService now shares a transaction-bound eligibility check with request and approval processing: it locks the savings account, checks the expected authenticated member for self-service requests, requires ACTIVE account/member/product and allow_withdrawal, and compares the exact integer-kobo amount with the current posted balance. Member/product shared row locks keep changes to those rules ordered with the transaction. Immediate staff payout uses the same check; parked submissions and each approval step check before advancing. Final payout checks again under the retained account lock. Rejection remains available for ineligible requests. Failed approvals roll back the approval trail, decision receipt and any payout. Successful original receipts still replay their old acknowledgement without creating another debit; ordinary authentication/permissions remain required.

Pending requests continue to deduct and reserve no funds. Distinct requests can coexist if each individually fits the posted balance; final concurrent payouts serialize on the account, so only currently funded payouts can commit. This preserves existing behavior rather than silently introducing a cooperative reservation policy. The member form explains it and inactive-product accounts are excluded from its eligible account list. Minimum retained balance, notice periods, collateral/loan exposure restrictions and reservation administration are not represented by the current savings-product schema and remain open; this slice does not invent fixed limits.

PostgreSQL regressions cover over-balance refusal/rollback and later funding with the same uncommitted key, staff/member product/account/member refusal, eligibility before every approval step, decision rollback and rejection after rule changes, depleted balances and original recovery after funding, concurrent distinct pending/final and immediate withdrawals, product-edit races and member read eligibility. Earlier pending/retry tests now fund their accounts so their original retry assertions remain valid under submission checks.

The new actual-browser journey verifies member form over-balance refusal without a recorded request or browser intent, two unreserved requests, staff payout refusal after depletion and rejection, product-rule changes before payout, original approval recovery after rule restoration, late receipt replay, exact balances/current status, mobile layout and reconciliation. Existing 15 browser journeys remain. Final remote results and local acceptance are separate recovery gates. Conservative 409 browser intents remain available for original recovery; this slice does not add a new discard/refusal protocol.

No migration, historical financial rewrite, production merge/deployment, or automatic recovery/discard of existing local requests.
